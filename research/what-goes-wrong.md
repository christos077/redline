# What Goes Wrong: Recurring Clause Types That Burn People (Redline customer discovery, Agent 2)

## Method note
- Web searches used: 11 of 12. Pages fetched: 9 of 15 (2 of the 9 returned nothing usable: the Freelancers Union nonpayment PDF was unreadable and the NIU deposit page returned 404).
- "Page-read" figures were quoted from a fetched page. "Snippet-only" figures appear only in a search-result summary and I did not open the page. They are labelled as such.
- Stopped at 8 sourced findings, as instructed. Status of the FTC rules is taken from the FTC pages I read.
- **Important:** no single dataset ranks clause types against each other. The ranking below is a judgment call. It combines magnitude and frequency of whatever number each clause type has, and the numbers measure different things (complaints per day, share of freelancers, share of attorneys). They are not comparable. Treat the ranking as indicative only.

## Findings

1. **Freelancer nonpayment is the best-quantified contract failure I found.** The Freelancers Union page says "One in every two freelancers had trouble collecting payment in 2014," "71% 'struggle to collect payment for work at least once in the course of their career,'" and "The average unpaid freelance worker loses almost $6000 annually – which accounts for 13% of their total income." (Page-read.) Caveat: 2014 data, published 2015. The search snippet of the full Freelancers Union report (not opened; PDF unreadable) said "34% were not paid at all for some portion of their work" among those with trouble; treat as snippet-only. Source: https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2015/12/10/costs-nonpayment/

2. **Subscription / auto-renewal complaints are high and rising.** FTC press release: the FTC "receives thousands of complaints about negative option and recurring subscription practices each year," and "in 2024 the Commission received nearly 70 consumer complaints per day on average, up from 42 per day in 2021." (Page-read.) The FTC did finalize a "click-to-cancel" rule in Oct 2024, but I did not verify its current status on a page I read, so I make no claim about whether it is in force. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring

3. **Lease fees and late fees are widespread, per tenant attorneys.** NCLC survey of 95 legal services and nonprofit attorneys (Nov-Dec 2022): "Almost all survey respondents (89%) reported that landlords impose rental application fees"; "Nearly as many (87%) stated that landlords charge excessive late fees"; also utility-related fees (73%), processing or administrative fees (68%), convenience fees (60%), insurance fees (59%), notice fees (56%). (Page-read.) Caveat: respondents are advocates, so this skews toward problem cases and is not a random renter sample. Snippet-only (not opened): a 2025 NLIHC National Renter Survey reportedly found 76.9% of renters of corporate or rental companies reported at least one of five junk fees, versus 41.1% of renters of individual landlords (https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/2025_National_Renter_Survey.pdf). Source: https://www.nclc.org/too-damn-high-new-report-examines-role-of-junk-fees-in-skyrocketing-rents/

4. **Security deposit disputes are repeatedly named as a top tenant complaint, but numbers are old.** Snippet-only: a 2013 Rent.com survey found "more than a quarter -- 26 percent -- of renters have been denied their security deposits," and 36 percent said their landlords offered no explanation (https://www.aol.com/news/2013-01-29-security-deposit-refund.html). Also snippet-only: a university legal office stated "the largest number of tenant complaints we receive involve the failure of landlords to return security deposits" (https://www.niu.edu/legal/law/landlord/deposit.shtml; this page returned 404 when I tried to open it, so treat as unverified). Also snippet-only: Ireland's Residential Tenancies Board data show deposit retention at 27% of disputes cases in 2020 and 16% in Q4 2023, behind rent arrears at 30% (https://rtb.ie/images/uploads/forms/RTB_Disputes_Summary_2023_Q4_Quarterly_-_V1.21.02.2024.pdf). This is Irish data, not US.

5. **Mandatory arbitration clauses are common and mostly unnoticed.** CFPB 2015 study: "card issuers representing more than half of all credit card debt have arbitration clauses" (as many as 80 million consumers); "banks representing 44 percent of insured deposits have arbitration clauses"; "companies invoked the arbitration clause to block class actions 65 percent of the time"; "more than 75 percent of consumers surveyed did not know whether they were subject to an arbitration clause"; under 7% understood that it barred lawsuits in court. (Page-read.) Caveats: the page notes the related CFPB rule was disapproved under the Congressional Review Act in 2017, the data are from 2015 and cover financial products only, and it measures prevalence and awareness, not complaint counts. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-study-finds-that-arbitration-agreements-limit-relief-for-consumers/

6. **Non-competes bind a large share of workers.** FTC: "An estimated 30 million workers—nearly one in five Americans—are subject to a noncompete." The page also says "The Noncompete Rule is not in effect and it is not enforceable." (Page-read.) This is prevalence, not a count of disputes or harm. The FTC's projected benefits ($524 per year in worker earnings; up to $194 billion lower health care costs over a decade) are projections from the rule, not measured complaints. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes

7. **Tenant-screening and rental-debt complaints are large but are not clause problems.** Snippet-only: CFPB logged roughly 26,700 tenant-screening complaints Jan 2019 to Sep 2022 (17,200+ about incorrect information) and 1,700+ rental debt complaints from Aug 2023 to year end; the CFPB also notes renter complaints about junk fees such as payment-processing charges required to pay rent. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/consumer-snapshot-tenant-background-checks/ (I did not open it). Included for context; I excluded it from the clause ranking because it concerns credit reporting, not contract language.

8. **Freelancer scope creep, IP, kill fees, long payment terms: qualitative signals only.** Snippet-only: a DEV Community post attributes to a "2025 Freelancers Union survey" that 67% of freelancers hit scope creep on their latest project, averaging 12.4 unpaid hours; I could not find the original, so this is unverified. A Freelancers Union blog (page-read) calls poorly defined scope "probably the single largest economic leak in freelance work" and says agreements should address "scope, deliverables, revision limits, payment timing, late fees, intellectual property ownership"; that page states it contains no statistics and does not mention kill fees. Practitioner checklists (search result summaries, vendor blogs, not authoritative) repeatedly flag broad IP assignment, net-60 payment terms with no late fees, missing kill fees, and unlimited revisions as risks. Sources: https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2026/05/13/the-most-common-freelancer-mistakes-and-why-they-keep-costing-people-real-money/ ; https://dev.to/prismforge/7-contract-clauses-every-freelancer-needs-before-signing-anything-3l5b

## What I could not find / ranking caveats
- **No cross-clause ranking by complaint frequency exists in what I found.** The ranking above is my judgment from non-comparable numbers. Ranks 1 and 2 could swap. Rank 7 is weakest.
- **Not found:** any quantitative data on IP assignment, indemnity, liability caps, kill fees, net-90 terms, unilateral termination, or unilateral change-of-terms (terms of service) as a source of complaints or disputes. These are unranked for lack of evidence, not because they do not matter.
- **Not found:** WorldCC "Most Disputed Terms" ranking (B2B). The WorldCC 2024 page I opened held no ranking. A search summary said limitation of liability, price/charges and indemnification are the most *negotiated* terms, which measures negotiation, not harm, and I did not verify it on the page.
- **Not found:** FTC or CFPB complaint breakdown by clause (e.g. share of complaints about auto-renewal vs. other terms); a US legal-aid "most common disputes" breakdown with percentages; Reddit thread counts (I did not search Reddit directly).
- **Dated data:** freelancer nonpayment (2014), security deposit (2013), arbitration (2015).
- **Self-selection and bias:** NCLC surveyed advocates; the CFPB arbitration study was disputed by critics in a search result I saw (snippet-only).
- **Regulatory status:** I confirmed from pages read that the FTC noncompete rule is not in effect and the CFPB arbitration rule was disapproved in 2017. I did not confirm the current status of the click-to-cancel rule.
- These are all US-leaning except the Irish RTB figures, which are for a different legal system.
